## 1. The claim I went looking to falsify, and could not

v7.9's changelog asserts the legal-model integration was *"verified against
three cases: a resolved measurement, a disputed-bracket refusal, and the legacy
path."* `harness_self_checks()` does not exercise `measure_with_category` with a
`commodity_class` **at all** — it tests hard-step exactness, homography
reprojection and the supported-extreme guard, and nothing else. So the claim had
no executable backing in the shipped code.

I wrote the test. **The claim is true.** All three cases pass, including the two
that matter most:

- The disputed 50–100 cm² bracket refuses with `THRESHOLD_DISPUTED`, reports no
  height, and **neither candidate value (`1.5` nor `2.0`) appears anywhere in
  the refusal text or the legal report.** The provenance sheet promises exactly
  this and the code delivers it.
- Legacy and integrated paths agree on `h` to the last binary digit.

This was an unverified claim, not a fabricated one. The distinction matters and
I am not going to blur it to make a point. What *was* wrong is that the test
wasn't kept, so nobody could re-check it after the next edit. It is kept now.

## 2. New findings, in the order they should worry you

### 2.1 ROI-boundary sensitivity is an unbudgeted ninth error term

**This is the most important thing in this document.**

The measured height depends on where the operator draws the box — not only
through *which marks* are inside it (the known decimal-point risk), but through
the *level estimate*. `levels_iterative` derives ink and substrate levels from
the ROI's own pixel population. Include more blank substrate and the substrate
level rises, the 50% midpoint rises, and every crossing moves.

Same glyphs, same pixels, only the margin changes:

| ROI | measured h (mm) |
|---|---|
| tight ink bounding box | 1.40187055 |
| +50 px substrate @0.92 | 1.40309883 |
| +150 px substrate @0.92 | 1.40426338 |
| +300 px substrate @0.92 | 1.40508017 |
| +50 px substrate @0.99 | 1.40421564 |
| +150 px substrate @0.99 | 1.40661342 |
| +300 px substrate @0.99 | 1.40825439 |

**Spread: 0.0064 mm.** For scale, that is larger than three of your eight budget
terms (`convention_bias_residual` 0.005, `defocus_psf_asymmetry` 0.005,
`fiducial_localisation` 0.0005 — the last by a factor of thirteen), and 3.8% of
U(k=2).

Three things make it worse than its size suggests:

1. **It is monotonic and signed, not random.** More substrate → taller reading →
   toward COMPLIANT. §7.1 commits this project specifically not to err in that
   direction. It is not an RSS term; it is a bias with an operator's hand on it.
2. **It is measured on a clean synthetic scene.** Real packaging has printed
   colour fields, gloss, and varied substrate. There is no reason to think
   0.0064 mm is the ceiling and good reason to think it is the floor.
3. **The Q&A card names "operator discipline in region selection" as a
   mitigation for the decimal-point problem.** That same discipline is now shown
   to be an error source in its own right. The mitigation and the defect are the
   same hand on the same mouse.

I searched every document for this. It is not in the eight terms, not in §15,
not in §6a's relative-term analysis, not in the four gaps added 2026-09-06, and
not on the Q&A card. `Scene.roi_box` exists only on the synthetic class;
`lm_capture.py` contains no ROI mechanism at all, and §8 line 1011 says as much.

**What I would do:** do not try to add a budget term for this before the 15th.
You cannot derive one in three days and a number invented under deadline is
exactly what §15 exists to prevent. Instead:
- Write it down in §15 as a *named, quantified, unbudgeted* term. "We found a
  ninth source, we measured it at 0.0064 mm on synthetic, it is not in U, and we
  are telling you before you ask" is a strong sentence in a room full of
  metrologists. It is a much weaker one after somebody else notices.
- Add one line to the Q&A card. I have drafted it in §5 below.
- On the rig, fix the ROI procedure rather than the number: same box size every
  time, drawn to the printed field's edge, not the ink's. A repeatable procedure
  converts a bias into something you could later *measure*.

### 2.2 `FLOOR_LIMITED` can never fire in production

`detect_spans` calls `mm_to_px(0.05, ppm, floor_px=2)`. At 30 px/mm,
`0.05 × 30 = 1.5`, `round(1.5) = 2`, and `2 < 2` is false — so it lands exactly
on the floor **without being clamped to it**. I swept it: the smallest ppm at
which `FLOOR_LIMITED` stops firing is **30.0 px/mm**, and `CaptureLimits.min_px_per_mm`
is **30.0**.

So below 30, `RESOLUTION` refuses anyway. At or above 30, `FLOOR_LIMITED` cannot
fire. **It has no reachable region of its own.** It is decorative in precisely
the way the brief already flags `max_illum_gradient` as decorative — a second
instance of a pattern you have already named once.

Two consequences:
- **Slide 5 should strike "`FLOOR_LIMITED` may be a second [confirmed firing]".**
  The answer is no, and structurally so. Better: this is a *finding*, and a
  sharp one. "We audited our own gates and found one that cannot fire" is the
  kind of thing that wins a Q&A.
- **§13a item 9 is resolved.** The contradiction was never real: the sampling
  sweep's `FLOOR-LIMITED` label is a `mm_to_px` return value printed by
  `sampling_test`, not a `Refusal` — that function never calls `capture_checks`.
  Patch 4's "0.067 mm at 30 px/mm" is also correct: `2 px ÷ 30 px/mm = 0.0667 mm`
  is the effective support either way. One statement asks *"was the value
  clamped"*, the other asks *"what is the value in mm"*. Both stand. Item 9 can
  be closed.

The margin is **zero**, though. Do not describe 30 px/mm as comfortably above the
floor — 29.9 px/mm clamps. And if anyone ever changes `min_width_mm` from 0.05
or `min_px_per_mm` from 30, this exact coincidence breaks silently.

### 2.3 Two different U values both print as `0.17`, on the same screen

- Verdict line: U at `max(h, TL) = max(1.4019, 1.0)` → **0.17321453 mm**
- Budget report: U at `H_REF_MM = 1.0` → **0.16634003 mm**

They differ by 4.1% and are indistinguishable at 2 d.p. The behaviour is
*correct* — evaluating U at `max(h, TL)` is a deliberate and well-argued choice —
but the display collapses two distinct quantities into one string, and §15's
"matching the hand computation to five decimals" refers only to the second one.

If a judge points at the verdict line and asks "is that the 0.16634 you quoted?",
the answer is no, and you want that answer rehearsed rather than discovered.

### 2.4 Two counts in the documents are wrong

- **Refusal codes.** Slide 5 says *"Nineteen codes in the file, fifteen of them
  about the measurement."* The file holds **twenty**. `LEGAL_MODEL_UNAVAILABLE`
  arrived with the 7.9 integration and was never re-counted. It is neither
  measurement nor legal in the slide's taxonomy — it is a plumbing refusal for
  when the import fails. The slide already documents this exact failure happening
  at "eight" on 2026-09-05. It has now happened again, one version later.
- **Gate provenance.** §8 says *"three derived, five placeholders"* and §15 says
  *"five of the eight numeric gates."* The ledger actually holds **nine: five
  placeholder, four derived.** The uncounted derived gate is `_FIDUCIAL_PICK_PX`.

Both are understatements, which is the harmless direction — but §15's whole
standing rests on its counts being right, and "we undercounted our own gates"
is a bad sentence to have to say in the room where you are arguing that your
discipline is the product.

## 3. The four bugs in `demo_script.py` — all fixed

The file's docstring claimed it *"wires the ALREADY-EXISTING pipeline together.
Nothing new was built to make this work."* That was false in four ways.

1. **`import lm_metrology_v7` against a file named `lm_metrology_v7.9.py`.**
   `ModuleNotFoundError` at line 18. The script had never run. *(Fixed by rename.)*
2. **`r.detail` on a `CaptureRefusal`.** That class carries `.cause`;
   `lm_metrology_v7.Refusal` carries `.detail`. `AttributeError` in BEAT 1, the
   first beat of the demo. *(Fixed.)*
   → Root cause worth naming: you have three refusal-shaped types and two
   different field names for the same concept — `Refusal.detail`,
   `CaptureRefusal.cause`, `LegalBlocker.cause`. That is what bit whoever wrote
   this file, and it will bite again at 2am.
3. **No `limits=`, so `expected_glyphs` was `None` — GLYPH_COUNT silently off
   for the whole demo.** The gate the counting-rule sign exists for, and job 4's
   predicted top offender, was disabled by omission. *(Fixed: it is now a
   required operator declaration with the counting rule quoted next to it.)*
4. **The entire camera frame passed as the ROI.** Every synthetic test crops to
   `Scene.roi_box` first. On a dry run with a synthetic full frame this did not
   crash — it silently returned a *different number* (1.4046 vs 1.4018), which is
   worse. *(Addressed: an operator ROI step with a headless fallback. See the
   caveat below.)*

**One thing I did not fix, deliberately.** `PLANARITY` and `NO_REDUNDANCY` still
cannot fire: both sit inside `if src_mm is not None` in `capture_checks`, and
this path passes no fiducials. Job 2's "done when" criterion — *"a real
photograph produces a verdict, and PLANARITY / NO_REDUNDANCY can actually fire"*
— **is not met and is not close.** That is rectification work, not wiring.
Inventing it three days out would be worse than saying so, and I have made the
script say so out loud rather than leave it to be discovered.

**Caveat on my own new code.** `select_roi()` is the one function in this project
with no self-test behind it. I wrote it today, it has never seen your camera, and
it is exactly the kind of untested-code-on-the-demo-path this project is careful
about everywhere else. Test it before the 15th or use the typed-coordinate
fallback.

## 6. The one piece of criticism I would give the project itself

The documentation discipline here is genuinely unusual and it is the project's
best asset. It also has a specific failure mode, and this audit found three
instances of it in one afternoon: **claims in prose that no longer match the
code, in a project whose entire pitch is that its claims match its code.**

The refusal count drifted at "eight", was caught, was documented as a lesson —
and then drifted again at "nineteen", by the same mechanism, one version later.
The gate ledger says nine and two documents say eight. A changelog asserted a
verification whose test was not kept.

None of these is dishonest and every one of them is the kind of thing a
Consumer Affairs judge could find in ninety seconds with your own documents open.
The fix is not more prose. It is that **every count in a document should be a
number the code can print.** `test_integration.py` is a start; the pattern it
should grow into is a test that fails when slide 5's numbers go stale.

That is the strongest version of this project's own argument, turned on itself.
