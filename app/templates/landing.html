Packaged Commodities Rules, 2011

It measures character height.
Most instruments only guess it.

A height in pixels becomes a height in millimetres only through a pixels-per-millimetre factor. With nothing of known size in the frame, that factor has to be assumed — and an assumed factor is wrong by however far the camera has moved. This instrument measures against a scale reference, states its uncertainty, and refuses to rule when the image cannot settle the question.

The method

One packet, three camera distances

An estimator calibrated once and then trusted spreads 0.42 mm across three distances and flips its verdict from compliant to deficient. A scale-referenced measurement spreads 0.0012 mm and returns the same answer every time.

This is a geometric fact, not a claim about anyone's software. It is why the scale reference is mandatory here and why there is no fallback that infers it from the image.
